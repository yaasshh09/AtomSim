What's new

Last updated 24 October 2026

Found something wrong, or want something added? Open an issue on GitHub. A physics mistake is the most useful report there is.

October 2026

  • Past argon: potassium and calcium, and a race between 3d and 4s configurations showing why scandium fills 4s although its 3d is deeper.
  • Hartree-Fock against experiment: Koopmans ionization energies for every atom from helium to calcium beside NIST's measured values.
  • The probability current: through the cross-section for a single state, and as moving arrows in the time-evolution view, with phase shown as colour.
  • Expectation values in the side panel, with a live virial check computed from the wavefunction itself, also for every What-If force law.
  • sp, sp² and sp³ hybrid orbitals, built from hydrogen's own n = 2 states.
  • Exact nodes marked on the cross-section, and a real-size scale bar.
  • The Born rule, one measurement at a time, in the radial view.
  • Time evolution of superpositions, with a builder for your own mix.
  • 3-D export (STL, PLY, GLB), a "Reproduce in Python" fold under every view, and "Cite this value" BibTeX on every provenance badge.
  • A plain light theme with a dark mode, gridlines on every plot, and a plot toolbar with PNG, SVG and CSV export.
  • Link previews drawn from the orbital each shared link opens.
  • Popular states are precomputed, and repeated requests come from a cache.
  • A guide to the public API.

September 2026

  • A layout for phones, with plots that fit them.
  • Physics notation is typeset instead of printed as plain text.
  • Every explanation in the app rewritten in a plainer voice.

August 2026

  • Hartree-Fock atoms in every view, sulfur and chlorine included, and the exclusion principle switchable off as a counterfactual.
  • Isosurfaces that enclose a chosen fraction of the probability.
  • The whole atom's radial electron density, and the two many-electron models compared on one axis.
  • Guided tours, and the public site.

July 2026

  • Hydrogen, exactly: clouds, cross-sections, radial functions, levels and spectra, each labelled with how it was computed.
  • Fine structure, the exact Dirac levels, the Zeeman and Stark effects and the 21 cm hyperfine line.
  • Line strengths, thermal populations, line shapes, optical depth and the curve of growth, checked against vendored NIST data.
  • Screened many-electron atoms, What-If physical constants and force laws, and the first Hartree-Fock solver, with exchange switchable off.
